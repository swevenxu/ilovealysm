# AFAR — Advanced Financial Accounting and Reporting
## CPA Exam Multiple-Choice Questions

## Q1
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** easy
**Question:** Under the acquisition method, which amount generally represents the consideration transferred in a business combination?
**A.** The acquiree's carrying amount of net assets
**B.** The acquisition-date fair value of assets transferred, liabilities incurred, and equity interests issued by the acquirer
**C.** The acquiree's historical cost of identifiable assets
**D.** The acquiree's book value of equity
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** The acquisition method measures consideration transferred at acquisition-date fair value. The other choices use carrying or historical amounts rather than the acquisition-date measurement basis.
**Source:** "consideration transferred in a business combination is measured at fair value"

---

## Q2
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** In a business combination achieved in stages, how is the acquirer's previously held equity interest generally treated at the acquisition date?
**A.** It remains at its original carrying amount
**B.** It is ignored in determining goodwill
**C.** It is remeasured to fair value, with the resulting gain or loss recognized as appropriate
**D.** It is measured at the acquiree's book value
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** A step acquisition requires the previously held interest to be remeasured to acquisition-date fair value. Keeping it at carrying amount would fail to apply the acquisition-date measurement basis.
**Source:** "previously held equity interest is remeasured to fair value at the acquisition date"

---

## Q3
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** If the acquisition-date fair value of identifiable net assets exceeds the consideration transferred plus the recognized amount of non-controlling interest, what should the acquirer do after reassessment?
**A.** Recognize goodwill
**B.** Recognize a bargain purchase gain
**C.** Defer the difference in equity
**D.** Record the difference as other comprehensive income
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** After reassessing the measurements, an excess of net identifiable assets over the acquisition-date amounts produces a bargain purchase gain. Goodwill arises from the opposite relationship.
**Source:** "any excess of the net identifiable assets over the consideration transferred and NCI is recognized as a gain"

---

## Q4
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** A parent owns 80% of a subsidiary. If the subsidiary reports ₱500,000 of profit for the period, what is the parent's share of that profit before other consolidation adjustments?
**A.** ₱100,000
**B.** ₱400,000
**C.** ₱500,000
**D.** ₱625,000
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** The parent's 80% interest gives it an ₱400,000 share of the subsidiary's ₱500,000 profit. The remaining ₱100,000 belongs to NCI.
**Source:** "profit or loss is attributed to the owners of the parent and to the non-controlling interests"

---

## Q5
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** Which statement best describes non-controlling interest in consolidated financial statements?
**A.** It is always presented as a liability
**B.** It represents the portion of a subsidiary's equity not attributable to the parent
**C.** It is eliminated entirely on consolidation
**D.** It is recognized only when the subsidiary is liquidated
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** NCI represents equity interests in a subsidiary that are not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent. It remains within consolidated equity rather than being eliminated as a liability.
**Source:** "non-controlling interest is the equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to a parent"

---

## Q6
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** Parent Co. sells inventory to Subsidiary Co. at a profit, and some of that inventory remains unsold at year-end. What consolidation adjustment is required?
**A.** Recognize the intercompany profit immediately
**B.** Eliminate the unrealized intercompany profit included in ending inventory
**C.** Increase consolidated inventory by the intercompany profit
**D.** Transfer the profit to NCI
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Consolidated financial statements treat the group as a single economic entity, so profit on inventory that remains within the group is unrealized and must be eliminated.
**Source:** "unrealized profit on intercompany inventory transactions is eliminated"

---

## Q7
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** In a downstream intercompany inventory sale from parent to subsidiary, the unrealized profit adjustment generally affects which party's reported profit?
**A.** The parent's profit
**B.** The subsidiary's profit
**C.** Only NCI
**D.** Neither entity's profit
**Correct:** A
**Explanation:** In a downstream sale, the parent is the seller. The unrealized profit therefore originated in the parent's income and is eliminated against the parent's profit.
**Source:** "downstream transactions are transactions from parent to subsidiary"

---

## Q8
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** hard
**Question:** A parent sells equipment to its subsidiary at a gain. The equipment remains within the group. Which consolidation treatment is appropriate?
**A.** Retain the gain because the sale was legally completed
**B.** Eliminate the intercompany gain and adjust depreciation to the amount that would have been recognized without the intercompany sale
**C.** Recognize the gain in OCI
**D.** Eliminate only the equipment's accumulated depreciation
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** The group cannot recognize an internal gain before the asset is effectively sold outside the group. Depreciation must also be based on the group's original carrying amount.
**Source:** "gains arising from transactions between group entities are eliminated"

---

## Q9
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** hard
**Question:** When a parent and subsidiary have intercompany bonds, what is the consolidation objective?
**A.** Report the bonds as external investments and debt
**B.** Eliminate the intercompany asset, liability, and related income and expense
**C.** Reclassify the bonds as equity
**D.** Recognize all intercompany interest as consolidated revenue
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Intercompany debt does not represent an obligation to or claim against an outside party. The related bond balances and income or expense are therefore eliminated in consolidation.
**Source:** "intercompany balances and transactions, including intercompany debt and interest, are eliminated"

## Q10

**A.** Functional currency
**B.** Presentation currency
**C.** Transaction currency

**Explanation:** Presentation currency is the currency in which the financial statements are presented. Functional currency is the currency of the primary economic environment.

**A.** The currency of its largest shareholder

## Q12
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** A foreign-currency monetary item is outstanding at reporting date. Under the usual closing-rate approach, which rate is used for translation?
**A.** Historical rate only
**B.** Closing rate
**C.** Average rate for the entire life of the item
**D.** Rate at the date of original invoice only
**Correct:** B
**Explanation:** Monetary items denominated in foreign currency are translated at the closing rate at the reporting date. Using the original transaction rate would ignore subsequent exchange-rate changes.
**Source:** "foreign currency monetary items shall be translated using the closing rate"

## Q14

**Difficulty:** hard
**Question:** A derivative contract has a positive fair value at reporting date. Before applying hedge accounting, how is the derivative generally recognized?
**A.** As an off-balance-sheet commitment
**B.** As a financial asset measured at fair value
**C.** As inventory
**D.** Only in OCI
**Correct:** B

## Q15

**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** A forward contract entered into for trading purposes changes in fair value by ₱40,000 during the period. Absent qualifying hedge accounting, where is the change generally recognized?
**A.** Profit or loss
**B.** Share capital
**C.** Inventory
**D.** Deferred tax only
**Correct:** A
**Explanation:** Derivative fair value changes generally go to profit or loss unless a specific hedge-accounting model applies. The other choices do not represent the normal location of trading derivative gains or losses.
**Source:** "a gain or loss arising from a change in the fair value of a derivative shall be recognized in profit or loss"

## Q16
**Format:** multiple_choice
**Difficulty:** medium
**Question:** Which derivative is commonly used to establish a right, but not an obligation, to buy or sell an underlying item at a specified price?
**A.** Forward
**B.** Future
**C.** Option
**D.** Swap
**Correct:** C
**Explanation:** An option gives the holder a right without imposing the corresponding obligation to exercise. Forwards and futures generally create contractual obligations for both parties.
**Source:** "an option gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell"

**A.** To express financial statements in terms of the measuring unit current at the reporting date
**B.** To convert all amounts into the parent's currency
**C.** To eliminate all non-cash assets
**D.** To recognize only nominal gains
**Correct:** A
**Explanation:** The central objective is to restate financial statements so that all amounts are expressed in terms of the measuring unit current at the reporting date.
**Source:** "financial statements shall be stated in terms of the measuring unit current at the end of the reporting period"
